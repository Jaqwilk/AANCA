Automated nucleus-annotation auditing

Which annotations deserve a second look?

A reproducible, group-safe framework that assigns review priority to each potentially inconsistent annotation. High-priority cases are recommended for expert review—without changing the source labels.

By Natan SmogórReleased 18 August 2026
Unstructured nuclei → ordered review queue
Interactive Three.js field

A model can identify labels that do not fit the patterns learned from the rest of a dataset. That is a triage signal—not a verdict.

AANCA tests whether that signal can move intentionally corrupted nucleus labels toward the front of a fixed expert-review queue. Because the benchmark knows exactly which labels were changed, ranking quality can be measured objectively.

The system never decides that an annotator was wrong. It preserves every source annotation and produces only a priority order for review by a qualified expert.

Scope. This presentation reports the completed frozen-feature study. Confirmatory CNN experiments, blinded expert review and external validation have not been performed. The accepted analysis is presented as exploratory because outcomes were inspected during recovery.
Research question

Can group-safe out-of-fold models find controlled label inconsistencies more efficiently than random review—and does correcting the highest-ranked labels improve downstream classification?

How the benchmark works

From source label to review priority.

  1. Preserve the original evidence

    Every nucleus keeps its patch-level group and pre-corruption class.

  2. Create known inconsistencies

    Labels are intentionally changed under four fixed mechanisms and saved separately.

  3. Predict without group leakage

    Out-of-fold models never see the scored nucleus or any nucleus from its source patch.

  4. Rank, then measure retrieval

    Suspicion scores order a fixed review queue; average precision measures how well it works.

  5. Leave the decision to an expert

    The output recommends what to inspect first. It never edits or diagnoses.

Group-safe splitting

Every split uses group_id, at least at source-patch level—never individual nuclei.

Out-of-fold ranking

Primary model-based audit scores come from group-safe predictions made on unseen groups.

Untouched reference fold

The final reference fold remains uncorrupted and unavailable for model selection or tuning.

Separate label states

pre_corruption_label, observed_label and all corruption metadata remain distinct.

Interpretation boundary

What this study supports—and what it does not.

Supported by this benchmark

  • Group-safe ranking retrieved injected label corruptions more efficiently than random review in the registered H1 comparisons.
  • The fixed hybrid exceeded self-confidence in all 12 registered H5 comparisons.
  • The complete workflow is reproducible from sealed, machine-readable evidence.

Not established here

  • That naturally occurring labels are wrong, or that a pathologist made an error.
  • That ranking automatically improves downstream classification—the H4 result was adverse.
  • Clinical validity, expert agreement or external generalisation.
